Flood mapping that survives clouds

Floods come with storms, and storms come with clouds — exactly when a flood map is needed, the optical satellite image is often covered. This demo compares two flood-segmentation models from my final-year project on a real flood scene from the Sen1Floods11 test split (never used in training):

  • Optical-only U-Net: sees only the Sentinel-2 optical image.
  • FusionNet: fuses the same optical image with Sentinel-1 radar (SAR), which sees through clouds. Both were trained with the same cloud-robustness recipe.

Drag the slider to cover the optical image with synthetic, fully opaque clouds (radar is unaffected — and the flood on the ground doesn't change, so the label stays the truth). The optical-only model loses the water under the clouds; the fusion model keeps finding it from radar. On the full test split (90 chips), water IoU at 100% opaque cloud: optical-only 0.06 (single training seed) vs fusion 0.64 (mean of 3 training seeds). With no clouds the two score 0.82 and 0.81.

How it works

Both models are U-Net-style segmentation networks with ResNet-34 encoders, trained on the hand-labelled Sen1Floods11 subset. The optical-only baseline takes the 13 Sentinel-2 bands. FusionNet runs a separate encoder per modality — radar (VV, VH) and optical — and fuses them at every scale, with bidirectional cross-attention at the two deepest levels, into one shared decoder. Both were trained with the same robustness recipe: synthetic clouds on the optical image plus random dropout of a whole modality, so the network cannot rely on optical alone.

The clouds here are synthetic and fully opaque: inside the cloud mask the ground signal is replaced entirely by a measured cloud spectrum, so nothing of the scene survives underneath. That is the stricter of the two variants in the project's robustness evaluation — real clouds are often semi-transparent, and water sometimes stays faintly visible through them. Each chip's cloud field is seeded from its chip id and does not depend on the coverage, so raising the slider grows the same cloud field instead of drawing a new one. Model selection used the validation split only; these chips are from the test split.

Water IoU is the intersection-over-union of the water class on the displayed chip, with unlabelled (no data) pixels ignored. The split-level numbers quoted above pool pixel counts over all 90 test chips, so they are not the average of single-chip values.

Precomputed, not live

This is a static page, so there is no model running in your browser. Every panel and every IoU was computed ahead of time by the real trained models (the seed-0 best.pt checkpoints), on CPU, for each of the 8 chips at each 10% cloud step, and exported as images by scripts/export_static_demo.py. The page just swaps the matching image in. The numbers are identical to the interactive Gradio version of this demo.
